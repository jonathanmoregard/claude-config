# B2B SaaS Brand + Marketing Playbook — State of the Art (2025–2026)

## The framework stack (each tool does one job)

- **April Dunford positioning (5 inputs), first artifact:** competitive alternatives → unique attributes → value → segments who care most → market category. Everything downstream needs the category frame. Exit gate: "[Product] helps [ICP] achieve [outcome] without [primary objection]."
- **Onliness statement (Neumeier) as a stress-test, not a tagline:** "Our [category] is the only one that [differentiator] for [audience] who [need], in the era of [context]." A conversation-forcer — run against the positioning, ~30 min.
- **Messaging house:** roof = core promise; 3–5 pillars = supporting claims; foundation = proof points; optional basement = purpose/values. Built from interviews (leaders + 2–3 customers + prospects); one approver — "deliver great messaging, not please everyone."
- **Category Entry Points (CEPs, Ehrenberg-Bass):** situational cues that make buyers think of the category — "when I need to schedule with a group across time zones," "when everyone keeps double-booking." Link the brand to **5–7 CEPs in customers' own words**, not one hero message. Each ad concept maps to one CEP, not features.
- **Voice spectrums, not adjectives:** mark positions on continua (Formal ↔ Casual, Serious ↔ Playful, Technical ↔ Simplified), one explanatory sentence each. Kills the "authoritative *yet* approachable" trap.

## Brand humor — two schools that don't mix

- **The wink (enterprise-safe): Mailchimp, Slack, Atlassian.** Mailchimp: "Our humor is dry. Our sense of humor is straight-faced, subtle, and a touch eccentric. We're weird but not inappropriate, smart but not snobbish. We prefer winking to shouting." Operational rule: **"If you're unsure, keep a straight face."**
- **The commitment (consumer-only): Liquid Death, Duolingo.** Andy Pearson (Liquid Death): "It's way more fun to never blink or never give a wink." Brand-as-character — write what the character would do in the scene, never break. Would break B2B trust.
- The schools don't mix; pick one.
- **Metamodern (Oatly):** postmodern self-awareness AND earnest sincerity held simultaneously — "we know you know we're selling something" + "we actually mean this." Oatly carton: "If this side of the carton bores you, please read no further."
- Playfulness signals *confidence* to enterprise buyers — provided the joke never obscures the claim. Mailchimp's correction: humor lives in marketing surfaces; the product gets clear, straight tone.

### Six practical subtle-humor rules

1. **The metamodern move:** pair one earnest sentence with one self-aware one — not sarcasm, both true. ("Group scheduling that's fast, private, and (yes, we know) not going to change your life. It'll just fix your Tuesdays.")
2. **Punch at the situation, never the reader.** Oatly punches at Oatly; Mailchimp at confusing marketing jargon.
3. **Straight-face default.** Don't force a joke. Voice is consistent; tone bends to context — order confirmations get none.
4. **One joke per page max on enterprise sales surfaces.** Homepage hero: dry allowed. Pricing: one warm micro-line. Enterprise contact form: zero.
5. **Character sheet, not adjectives:** define the brand as a person — beliefs, opinions, dislikes, favorite phrases, phrases they'd never say. Ask "what would [character] say?"
6. **Test humor on the sceptical buyer, not the enthusiast.** If the VP-of-Ops persona rolls their eyes, cut it.

## Solo-founder build order (don't skip ahead)

- **Phase 1 — Clarity (wk 1–4):** (1) hyper-specific ICP, firmographic + psychographic; (2) customer-language extraction — 5–10 interviews, exact phrases ("people click on ads that sound like their own thoughts"); (3) Dunford positioning doc, one-sentence gate; (4) onliness stress-test + 5–7 CEP list.
- **Phase 2 — Foundation (wk 3–6):** (5) one-page voice guide — character sketch, 3–4 spectrums, 5 do/5 don't examples, "phrases we'd never say"; (6) messaging house; (7) **copy bank** — 1 hero line + 3 variants, 5 subheads, 10 CTA variants, 5 social bios, 3 taglines, 3 elevator pitches (10/30/60s), 5 objection responses. The steal-from-all-year file.
- **Phase 3 — Assets (wk 5–10):** (8) one landing page — hero answers what/who/why in 5 seconds, name paired with category cue + one CEP-relevant promise; (9) 3–5 ad concepts, one CEP each, test wording not creative; (10) 3–5 welcome emails.
- **Phase 4 — One channel (wk 8+):** pick ONE — bottom-of-funnel SEO ("[competitor] alternative"), cold outreach (500–1,000 verified contacts, pain-keyed 3-email sequence), or founder build-in-public. **90-day rule:** run it 90 days before evaluating; add a second only when the first is predictable.
- **Ordering mistakes:** copy before positioning → generic copy; content calendar before pipeline → "content didn't work"; three channels at 30% instead of one at 100%; voice guide before language extraction → voice becomes aesthetic, not asset.

## Naming essentials

- **Structure:** 5–8 characters, 1–3 syllables (Slack, Zoom, Notion, Figma, Linear). Pronounceable on first read by non-native English speakers (avoid TH-sounds, silent letters, heavy consonant clusters). **Spellable after hearing once** — read aloud to 10 people of different first languages; 3+ misspellings = reconsider.
- **Domain:** .com for B2B; .io/.co for dev tools only; enterprise distrusts .ai/.io otherwise. Avoid diacritics (å/ä/ö) globally or plan an ASCII fallback that owns search (Häagen-Dazs precedent: invented faux-Nordic, ASCII in search).
- **Styles that work:** invented/coined (Vercel, Figma — higher launch cost, higher long-term equity, ~3.2× trademark approval vs descriptive), evocative real word (Notion, Loop), foreign-flavored coin, metonym (Slack). Check false-friends across major languages.
- **SMILE/SCRATCH (Watkins):** SMILE = Suggestive, Meaningful, Imagery, Legs, Emotional — aim 4/5. SCRATCH = Spelling, Copycat, Restrictive, Annoying, Tame, Curse, Hard-to-pronounce — cut anything hitting 2+.
- **Verb-ability:** test "Let's [name] Tuesday." Verb-able names see ~23% higher word-of-mouth; if awkward, give buyers a category verb via tagline instead.
- **Tagline carries the category cue for coined names** (buyers can't categorize an invented name in 3 seconds): Name + category cue or Name + one-outcome promise. Slack "Where work happens"; Vercel "Develop. Preview. Ship." Pattern: `[Name] — group scheduling that doesn't ask everyone twice.`
- Trademark: clear Nice classes 9 + 42 (EUIPO; Bolagsverket company-name collision separately; PRV via TMview; US via aggregators until attorney-cleared).
- Wordmark: one strategic idiosyncrasy is the whole quirk budget; avoid gradient icons, smiley-O logos, purple-teal duotone (overfished in 2026).
