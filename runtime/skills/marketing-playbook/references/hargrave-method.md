# Hargrave Method — "Marketing for Hippies" Frameworks

Tad Hargrave's ethical-marketing system for founders who reject hype. Primary source: marketingforhippies.com.

## Seven Fundamentals of Ethical Marketing

Ethics, Space, Niche, Point of View, Business Model (Offers), Hubs, Sharing.

## Core agenda: "Is it a fit?"

- Replace the hidden agenda "get the sale" with a transparent one: *find out whether this is a fit*. "If your hidden agenda is to *get the sale*, your marketing will always feel off in some way... If your entirely transparent agenda is to see if it's a fit, your marketing will consistently feel refreshing, respectful and wonderful."
- **Three roles of marketing:** (a) attract attention, (b) **filter** — actively exclude people you're not right for (a first-class outcome), (c) **lower the risk** of the next step.
- Slow marketing: roots before fruits; word-of-mouth compounds; no pressure, no scripts. The people who love your work market you — make sharing easy.

## Point of View (POV) marketing

- POV = your perspective, philosophy, take, world-view, principles, voice, values, map, compass, route.
- **Diagnosis / Prognosis / Prescription:** (1) what's *actually* going on, (2) what happens if they do/don't act, (3) what you propose. Load-bearing line: **"If they don't buy your diagnosis, they won't buy your prescription."**
- **Island A → Island B:** A = the state clients arrive in; B = where you take them. "Your point of view is the map you use to get from one island to the other." A niche is a before→after journey, not a demographic.
- **Deliverable: a "My Philosophy" page.** Opening prompts: *"Start by talking about the BS you see in your industry that you're sick of. Don't be afraid to show your emotion and that you care."* Pair with: *what has broken your heart about this industry, and what did that heartbreak clarify?*
- POV is not: a promise of result, empathy, a values statement, or your message (more compressed). The fear of publishing a POV is about colleagues/family/friends, not ideal clients; name that up front.
- Signature workshop is the main POV vehicle: ~90% perspective + a taste of experience; the pitch is just "if this resonates..."

## Niche = role in the marketplace

- "Your niche is not simply your brand or target market but your role in the community." Operational: *what's the role in the marketplace you want to be known for?* Six overlapping circles (who/what/where/when/why/how) — "who" is only one.
- **Niching Spiral** — a seven-step process (fully enumerated in his book *The Niching Nest*). Anchor moves: journal the good/bad/ugly/inspiring of your current market; Artist's spiral (niche via body of work) vs Entrepreneur's spiral (via market analysis); three levels of a workable niche.
- **Niching interviews** — the pivot move: sit one-on-one with someone who best represents the target; test your best offer's language on them. Iterate until you can predict their language. Test: can you tell their stories? "Your deepest wound is often a doorway to your truest niche."

## Trust: Relevance / Credibility / Value (via Robert Middleton)

- **Relevant** — "is this of use to me and my situation?" → established by **niche**. Judged in ~3 seconds.
- **Credible** — "can I trust this? all sizzle and no steak?" → established by **POV**. Build credibility *before* relationship.
- **Valuable** — "is this worth the time and money?" → established by **offers**.
- A struggling business lacks one or more of these three.
- **Three risk-lowering levers:** (1) reputation (biggest reducer; word-of-mouth), (2) education (POV marketing in its safety role), (3) real, honest guarantees.

## Pink spoon + hubs

- **Pink spoon** = a genuinely substantive free taste (Tad gives a day-long workshop free); thin lead-magnet checklists fail. **Forwardability test: would people spontaneously forward this? If not, it's underwhelming.**
- **Hubs** — people/venues who already have your ideal clients' attention. "Hubs are everything in marketing. They make any tactic work better." Four hub-identification questions:
  1. Where do they already spend their time, money and attention?
  2. What groups are they part of?
  3. What social media do they use?
  4. Who are the people they're already connected to whom they respect?
- Ask at every workshop: "who else in town should I talk to?" — every attendee becomes a scout. Workshop structure (~60 min): story → POV → breakthrough experience → clear next step, feeding a "Next-Step Ladder" of offers.

## Seven criteria of a good message

1. A New Idea (or an old one said provocatively new) 2. Short (~10 words) 3. Provocative (invites inquiry) 4. Repeatable (chorus-like) 5. Simple (profound implications) 6. Well Crafted (exact right words; test them) 7. You (authentic; you'd explore it for years). Everything — design, names, pricing — expresses the message.

## Runnable exercises

1. "Is it a fit?" reframe — before any marketing action: closing, or fit-finding? Redesign if closing.
2. Four founding questions: What are we doing? For whom? Helping them with what? What do we stand for?
3. Industry rant — "BS you're sick of" + "what broke your heart" → My Philosophy opener.
4. Diagnosis/Prognosis/Prescription drill — three paragraphs; test they buy (a) before pitching (c).
5. Island A → B statement — one paragraph per island, then the route.
6. "You know you have found a good target market when ___" fill-in.
7. Good/bad/ugly/inspiring journal on your current market.
8. Ideal-client-trait retro — list favourite past clients, extract 6–10 traits, use as filter.
9. Niching interviews (protocol above).
10. "Deepest wound → truest niche" journaling.
11. Seven-criteria message checklist.
12. RCV audit — rank every asset: Relevant? Credible? Valuable?
13. Hub-identification worksheet (four questions).
14. Hub-scouting ask at every talk: "who else should I talk to?"
15. "Are you sure?" checkout ritual — ask at point of purchase; filters buyer's remorse.
16. Signature workshop: story → POV → breakthrough → next step.
17. Pink-spoon self-test: would people forward it?
18. "Confused mind says no" — read aloud; confusion = no conversion.
19. Overnight sanity check — if it feels slimy or wouldn't fly in a friendship, don't use it.
